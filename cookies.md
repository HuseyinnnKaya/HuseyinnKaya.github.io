*What web cookies are, how they work, and why they matter for your privacy.*

Based on the Turkish article "Çerezleri Kabul Ediyor Musunuz? Nedir Bu Çerezler?" by Beyza Gizem Yıldırım. The text below is my own summary in English.

---

## 1. A question to start

You search for a coffee machine in the morning. In the evening, coffee machine ads follow you on social media.

**How does the internet "remember" you? The answer is cookies.**

---

## 2. What is a cookie?

A cookie is a small piece of data that a website stores in your browser. Cookies help websites in three main ways:

- **Remember you:** they keep you logged in, so you do not type your password every time.
- **Remember choices:** they keep items in your shopping cart and settings such as language.
- **Count visitors:** each cookie has an ID, so one person visiting many times counts as one visitor.

---

## 3. How does it work?

1. You visit a website. Your browser sends a request.
2. The server answers and sends a `Set-Cookie` header with a unique ID.
3. Your browser saves the cookie on your device.
4. On the next request, the browser sends the cookie back, so the site knows you.

The web itself is "stateless". Cookies give it a memory.

---

## 4. Types of cookies

| Type | How long? | Who sets it? |
|---|---|---|
| **Session** | Until you close the browser | The site you visit |
| **Persistent** | Days, months or years | The site you visit |
| **First-party** | Either | The site in the address bar |
| **Third-party** | Either | Another company, for example an ad network |

Third-party cookies can follow you across different websites. This is what makes ad tracking possible.

---

## 5. Why "cookie"?

- **Before:** in Unix computing, a "magic cookie" was a small token passed between programs.
- **1994:** Lou Montulli, a programmer at Netscape, adapted the idea for web browsers. Online shops needed a way to keep shopping carts without filling server memory.
- **Today:** cookies are everywhere, and so are the cookie banners that ask for your permission.

---

## 6. Privacy and the law

Cookies can be useful, but they can also collect personal data.

- **Europe:** GDPR and the ePrivacy rules. Sites must ask for consent for non-essential cookies and explain what they do.
- **Türkiye:** KVKK (Personal Data Protection Law). Sites must inform users and respect their choices.

That is why you see a notice and a cookie policy on almost every website.

---

## 7. What can you do?

- Read the banner. Choose **"Reject"** or **"Only necessary"** when you can.
- Clear cookies from your browser settings from time to time.
- Block third-party cookies in your browser.
- Use private (incognito) mode for sensitive searches.

---

## 8. Conclusion

Cookies give the web a memory. They make sites convenient, but they also need rules and your attention.
